## Q1. "If scratch-off physical already does this, why would anyone use a digital version?"

The sharpest question in the file. The Adventure Challenge proved people pay ~$50 for surprise-date + journal capture in one artifact. The research claims digital's edge is the archive (moat), resurfacing (Timehop), and ambient presence — but those are *retention* features, not *acquisition* reasons. Nobody has demonstrated that a digital reveal/commitment loop converts a first-time user. The honest position: **we have proof of the job (manufactured surprise dates) and zero proof of the digital form.** The form-factor decision (synthesis gate #1) is not a detail — it is the concept's existence question. Any plan that proceeds digital-first without a validation gate on the reveal loop is building on [OPEN].

## Q2. "Why wouldn't iMessage + a shared album + Instagram Close Friends do this already?"

They do the capture-and-share half, badly but sufficiently. What they don't do is *manufacture* the moment — the mission, the commitment device, the reveal. The research's answer: the game is an excuse engine ("the card told us to do it"). Evidence this excuse has market value: Adventure Challenge's 1.6M claimed copies [SECONDARY], WNRS's TikTok-scale virality [SECONDARY]. But note the gap: both are physical, social, gift-driven purchases. The digital excuse engine is unproven. Also note: Between tried to own the private-1:1 space and ended in harvest mode — the platform gravity (iMessage/WhatsApp) is real.

## Q3. "Asymmetric motivation killed every couples app. Why would this game be different?"

It might not be. This is the finding the research is most confident about *and* the problem it least solves. Every couples app died on "he stopped doing it after 3 days." The research's prescribed answer — "loop must complete when one partner is passive" — is a requirement, not a solution, and it collides head-on with finding #3 (bonding needs *synchronous joint action*). If one player is passive, there is no joint action; if joint action is required, the passive player kills it. **This contradiction is unresolved in the research.** Any honest plan must name which side gives: either the bonding core tolerates passivity (unproven), or the game accepts it only works for already-motivated pairs (smaller market, gift-driven acquisition).

## Q4. "Who pays, and why — really?"

The research says one-time $39–69, gift-framed. Evidence: physical decks. But a *digital* one-time unlock at that price is a different product with thinner proof (Love Nudge's $9.99 IAP is adjacent but a different job). The physical-goods upsell (photobooks/prints) is proven via FamilyAlbum — but FamilyAlbum gives the core away free at 16M-install scale first. The pricing hypothesis (H1 in track 3) is the weakest of the labeled hypotheses: it extrapolates physical pricing to a digital product. Flag: do not let "$39–69 test band" harden into a fact.

## Q5. "Who writes the missions forever?"

Content exhaustion at 6–12 months is documented (Agapé [SECONDARY], WNRS 365 month-8 repetition [SECONDARY]). The research offers "real-world missions don't exhaust" as a structural hope — labeled [OPEN], correctly. But press it: missions still need authoring, novelty calibration, difficulty/budget calibration (Tryuunion reviews complain about samey/hard missions [SECONDARY]), and darkness calibration (users literally throw out bad cards [SECONDARY]). "Real-world" doesn't remove the authoring burden; it changes its shape. UGC/community supply is named as an escape valve but has zero evidence in this category. The content-supply question is a company-level risk, not a feature.

## Q6. "Your psychology is all lab tasks. Does any of it survive the phone?"

Partially. Peak-end, photo-impairment, and retelling effects are about human memory generally — medium-agnostic enough to trust directionally. The synchrony findings (Wiltermuth & Heath: marching in step; Hove & Risen: finger-tapping) are *embodied, in-person* manipulations. Whether tapping "3-2-1-GO" on two phones produces the same bonding is [OPEN] and the transfer assumption is doing real work in finding #3. The missions themselves are in-person (that's the point — real-world moments), so the synchrony core may survive *because the game is played in person with the phone as accessory, not as the venue*. That reframes the product: phone as mission-dealer and capture device, not as the play space. The research doesn't quite say this out loud — it should.

## Q7. "What did we assume about the audience?"

We treated "friends, family, date-mates" as one audience. The market doesn't: Adventure Challenge ships separate couples/friends/family editions; Between is strictly 1:1; FamilyAlbum is family-private. Bonding mechanics (synchrony, shared challenge) likely transfer across all three, but acquisition (gifting vs. parenting utility), willingness to be silly (date-mates vs. grandparents), and session shape differ. The research under-examined family/kids and friend-group dynamics — almost all app evidence is couples. **The "one game for three relationships" assumption is unvalidated** and the products suggest segmented editions.

## Q8. "What contradicts what?"

- **Joint action (psych) vs. passive-partner survival (market):** see Q3. Unresolved.
- **Ritual enhances (Vohs) vs. streaks create guilt (Paired study):** same mechanism, opposite outcomes depending on framing. The research resolves it as "ritual without punishment" — a design constraint, not a contradiction, but the line is thin and untested.
- **Capture helps (Barasch: volitional capture boosts visual memory) vs. capture hurts (Henkel: mindless capture impairs):** resolved by intention — but "intentional" is doing heavy lifting; the game must *manufacture* intention via the capture beat. That's a design burden the research names but doesn't solve.
- **Short sessions (peak-end) vs. long archive (moat):** resolved as different layers (session vs. product). Genuine, no conflict.
- **Challenge bonds (Bastian) vs. users veto social cost (Ultimate Game reviews):** resolved by keeping challenge inside the group. The calibration line (how much embarrassment/effort is bonding vs. off-putting) is [OPEN].

## Q10. "What would kill this concept outright?"

Three falsifiers, in order:
1. A pilot showing the digital reveal/commitment loop doesn't change behavior (users peek, don't commit, or the novelty dies in week 2) — kills the digital form.
2. A pilot showing the passive partner still won't tap/guess even at near-zero effort — kills the asymmetric-motivation requirement, and with it the addressable market.
3. Content economics: if mission authoring can't sustain novelty past month 6 at a viable cost, the product is a one-season toy — which is fine for a $49 physical deck (re-buy next edition) but fatal for an app.

## Assumptions ledger (held vs. dropped)

- HELD: anticipation/reveal drives action and encoding (triple convergence).
- HELD: the archive is the retention moat; portability is trust (converged).
