## Art questions (answered from play)

- **Composition:** Iona's frame centers the keeper and the lamp; everything the player needs is at the frame's edges (page left, plate right, notch by the lamp). Mara's workbench centers the three objects in a row — the eye reads them as a sentence.
- **Silhouette:** The keeper's blue coat against fog is the game's most readable image; the lamp is the second. In Mara, the lightbox's layered sheets are the readable shape.
- **Text / scale:** The harbour-mark texts are short and procedural ("Aim EAST", "Short · long · short") — they read as instructions, not lore. The letter is the only long text, and it earns its length by arriving last.
- **Clue fairness:** Both marks are findable by leaning (the game teaches leaning before it asks for marks). The notch is the least fair clue — same edge as the plate, no prompt until the return visit names it.
- **Acknowledgment / motion:** The signal's answering light is the game's acknowledgment; the lightbox's moving layers are Mara's. Both are visible, immediate, and wordless.
- **Style / action:** The style is quiet and painterly; the actions are quiet too (lean, read, brace, fit, step). Nothing in the art asks the player to hurry.
- **Memory:** The wedge is the memory object: an ordinary scrap that the return visit reinterprets. "Someone has kept this ordinary scrap for a long time" (first sight) → "why I kept that little wedge" (return) → the letter (meaning).
- **Cue redundancy:** Bearing appears three ways (page text, scene header "Bearing · EAST", lamp panel "East — old breakwater"); rhythm appears twice (plate, flash-pattern panel). The redundancy is why the signal is solvable without notes.
- **Sound:** Not assessed — the play runs were silent (no audio evidence captured). [OPEN]
- **Social authorship:** Single-player; the "author" is the keeper (Iona) or the painter (Mara) — the player assembles what they left behind.

## Evidence strips

**Strip 1 — clue → theory → action → confirmation (Iona):** The tucked page says aim EAST (clue) → the player theorizes the ferry comes from the breakwater (theory) → sets bearing East and flashes Short-Long-Short (action) → "The ferry is safely home" (confirmation).

**Strip 2 — before → acknowledgment → settled result (Mara):** Before: three separate sheets, "registration notch 0". Acknowledgment: at notch 3 the window edges meet and the layers visibly align. Settled result: "The picture comes together" with its three-sentence invitation.

**Strip 3 — first appearance → later reinterpretation (the wedge):** First appearance: "A little wooden wedge rests below it. Someone has kept this ordinary scrap for a long time." Later: "Would you like to know why I kept that little wedge?" → brace the shade → fit the wedge → the letter: "I was frightened every night. I turned it on anyway." The wedge was never a tool; it was a biography.
