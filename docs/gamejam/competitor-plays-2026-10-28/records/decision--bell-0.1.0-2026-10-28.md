# The Thirteenth Bell 0.1.0 — Concept Decision Brief (2026-10-28)

**Demo:** The Thirteenth Bell 0.1.0 (Contrast). **Audience:** the 20 November 2026 release gate. **Evidence:** first-hand full loop (witness → anchor → Loop 2 → prepare → Mina at the well, [REAL]), competitor coverage (`coverage--bell-0.1.0-2026-10-28.md`), journey + art records. Third-person throughout.

## §1 — Why play it

Three visits before the thirteenth bell. Bell asks a player to predict what changes by noon, travel the town, and — the part that makes it different — *see* the thing happen. A report is not seeing. Arriving late is not seeing. Only an eyewitness clue survives midnight, and only a clue carried through midnight can change the next loop: the side passage, prepared in Loop 2 Dawn, brings Mina and her crate to the sheltered market well in Loop 2 Noon — "The main alley is still blocked; the changed route, not a changed crowd, brought her here." The demo proves the candidate Contrast's loop first-hand, end to end, in one session (bell19). What it also proves, five times over (three runs stalled in Noon, two at Midnight), is that the game hides its own advance rule — and a first-time player stalls on it.

## §2 — Why choose it on this platform

| Competitor | What it does | Why Bell instead |
|---|---|---|
| ROLLBACK (free browser) | Loop-deduction; notebook remembers everything; 8 endings | ROLLBACK's notebook is Bell's journal — but ROLLBACK *shows* the notebook as a first-class object and names its loop rule up front. Bell's witness rule is stricter and more interesting: only seeing counts. |
| Core Rush (free browser) | Loop-mining; the loop is a secret to discover; 12 hidden field notes | Core Rush hides its loop; Bell states its loop. The field notes are Core Rush's eyewitness clues — knowledge hidden in the world. Bell's clues are *earned* by being there. |
| Blue Prince ($29.99) | Puzzle-roguelike; a house that rearranges daily | The daily-reset reference: players accept a loop when the space changes. Bell's Loop 2 town (masks, the road climbs into the sky) is the same move. |
| Until Then ($19.99) | Narrative adventure; a day that will not repeat cleanly | The loop can be emotional, not just mechanical. Bell's Mina is the same bet — the loop matters because a person is in it. |
| Duck Detective ($9.99) | Deduction; observe, deduce, accuse | The player is told what counts as evidence. Bell's boundary panel ("A report or a late visit is not the same as seeing it happen") is the same move — the one place Bell *does* state its rule. |
| Tactical Breach Wizards ($19.99) | Tactics; rewind, plan-then-execute | The prediction idea: declare, then test. Bell's prediction is the same — but Bell never shows the plan's cost until the player pays it. |

No competitor in the six makes seeing-something-happen the only way to change the next loop. That is the differentiator.

## §3 — Distinctive experience (three candidates)

**Candidate A — the witness is the product.** Only an eyewitness clue survives midnight; only a prepared passage changes the route. Supported: the full loop ran first-hand — the marks → the clue → the anchor → Loop 2 → the preparation → Mina at the well. *Keep.*

**Candidate B — the prediction alone is the product.** The player declares what changes by noon and is graded on it; the witness is only the way to test. Rejected on play evidence: a prediction without a witness earns nothing — the game grades the forecast only after a registered visit tests it, and the Loop 2 payoff needs the eyewitness clue, not the prediction. The prediction is the loop's scoring verb, not a product on its own. *Reject as standalone; keep as the scoring inside A.*

**Candidate C — the altered town is the product.** Rejected as the *primary* experience: Loop 2's town (masks, the sky-climbing road, the bellkeeper where no street existed) is vivid, but it is the *result* of the witness, not the game. A player who never witnesses never sees it. *Reject as primary; keep as the payoff.*

## Proposed decision

**Choose Bell for the 20 November gate — conditional on one fix.** The candidate Contrast's loop is proven end to end; the headline payoff (Mina at the well) is proven first-hand. The condition: **one in-game line stating the advance rule** (a visit registers only on a scene action; the prediction must be tested; Dusk advances via the boundary bell; midnight needs a confirm click), then re-test with a fresh player. Five agent runs stalled on it (three in Noon, two at Midnight); a first-time human player hits the same wall. The paired human gate (at least 4/6 both explain cause AND predict before the outcome, 4/6 both earn the action uncoached AND explain Fracture, 3/6 recall a specific Mina outcome after a predeclared neutral break of at least two minutes) cannot pass while the rule is hidden. Run the gate after the fix, before 20 November. Out of scope for this round (unplayed, not done): the Original run, the previous-Contrast comparison, and Fracture 4.
