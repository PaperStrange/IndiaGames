# Vivid browser comparison — six actual-play records

Observed on 21 October 2026 by coding agents. **Six titles have core gameplay evidence across five broad families. Three declared observation units were completed; three intended endpoints remain open.** These are not six completed games, human sessions or a finished market study. The [coverage ledger](coverage.json) defines every count, and the [synthesis answers the three product questions](SYNTHESIS.md) with testable hypotheses.

The completed units differ deliberately: a management game's two-day loop, a narrative path, and a self-directed construction task. They are not interchangeable completion or enjoyment metrics. Two of the six titles share the narrative family; the roster also includes four titles outside it. Captures, input logs, assistance, mistakes and stopping/re-entry conditions are in each record. The coordinator reviewed representative original captures from all six titles; screenshot inspection is not another play session.

## Method and sources

Read the [group A method and sources](group-a/README.md), [group B method](group-b/README.md), [group B attributed criticism](group-b/criticism.md) and the [Six Cats critic cross-check](group-a/six-cats-under/README.md#critic-cross-check-read-after-the-attempt). Criticism of a full desktop or Switch edition is not evidence about the browser demo. Creator interviews and reviews sharpen questions; they do not increase the play-session count.

The task-scoped request relay retained upstream TLS verification. No hidden game state, save injection or success function advanced a competitor. Screenshot-guided aiming, programmed key cadence, public hints and walkthroughs are disclosed. Shared compute, screenshot latency and unavailable audio limit this method, especially for timed actions. Elapsed tool time is not player completion time, and repeated automation misses do not prove a game is difficult or unfun.
