## Assistance and measurement limits

- I read the preceding agent's partial journey before starting, including the hat → shouting square → fearful circle strategy. This was a follow-up investigation, not blind discovery.
- The first Play click and ordinary photograph used fixed coordinates. Later aiming used screenshots only: visible patches, grey hair pixels, and nearby white head shapes. The helper then issued an ordinary mouse click. It did not read game variables, inspect gameplay source, invoke progression functions, pause the simulation, or change rules/assets.
- The initial Python image-analysis invocation took about 2.5 seconds in a local timing check. Moving the grey-pixel path away from an unnecessary import reduced that analysis invocation to about 0.26 seconds. These are helper timings, not game performance measurements. Screenshot capture, tool turns and the shared browser environment introduced further delay.
- The final nearby-subject attempt used a short burst of three visible-image analyses; two produced clicks, one declined to click. Only the resulting visible frame was interpreted. A click emitted while a previous photo was resolving does not establish a new accepted photo.
- Public responses were delivered through the task-scoped trusted proxy transport described in the [group method](../README.md). TLS verification remained enabled using the supplied CA. No certificate-ignore flag, persistent trust change, account access or paid content was used.
- `driver.mjs` and `visible-match.py` record the research method. `inputs.jsonl` is an observation log, not a deterministic replay: characters move, the early helper was refined during the session, and only twelve selected images are retained here. The original screenshot path and actual capture time remain in `record.json` when a selected image was renamed.

The misses do **not** establish that WBWWB is difficult, inaccessible, poorly paced, or unfun for human players. This method was poorly suited to brief moving-target events. Conversely, seeing a scripted crowd response does not establish unrestricted simulation, broad market demand or lasting emotional impact.
