## Question before play

What makes a one-action game hold attention, and when does taking a picture become an act that changes a story? For Vivid, the corresponding question is whether inspecting and placing an object can carry narrative meaning instead of simply advancing text.

## Design hypotheses worth testing in Vivid

1. A small action can carry a large story consequence when the result is visible in the same scene. Iona's chosen placement should visibly persist, not only appear in a summary paragraph.
2. Repeating the same verb can change its emotional meaning. Bracing a lamp should first be a concrete action, then make the earlier worn notch meaningful.
3. Recognition needs a clear causal chain. A participant should be able to point at the changed object and explain what they did to cause it, without being asked whether they “felt immersed.”
4. Avoid importing this game's timed capture pressure into Vivid's care-focused interaction. A latched brace and select-then-place alternative let attention carry the scene without a reflex gate.

These hypotheses do not establish human enjoyment, preference, retention or uniqueness in the market. WBWWB itself is evidence against claiming that visible consequences or one-button narrative are unprecedented.
