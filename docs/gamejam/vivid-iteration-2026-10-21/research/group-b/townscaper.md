## Intention

The first contact tested whether clicking a polished miniature makes the next action discoverable. For the resumed session, the self-assigned composition is a small harbour landing attached to the supplied courtyard, with a blue annex. Success means making a recognisable extension, revising one part, choosing a final view, leaving and checking what survives re-entry. It does not mean “beating” this open-ended toy.

## Observed first contact

The loader first showed a progress bar and an animated Townscaper title, then a ready-made small yellow-and-red courtyard on water. A vertical colour palette and settings icon were visible. Two clicks on the water at the front-left created/extended a paved landing, with a small splash response. The structure changed immediately without a separate confirmation dialog. The target under the pointer moved as the geometry changed, so repeating the same screen coordinate did not simply stack a house.

I closed this tab while concentrating on WBWWB; it was not left rendering during Vivid QA. No save or recovery conclusion follows from closing a tab alone. Before resuming I read the PC Gamer review of the full desktop edition, including its description of left-click construction and right-click deletion. The resumed control discovery is therefore **assisted by that public description**, not wholly blind.

## Early design hypothesis

Tactile appearance earns an invitation to touch; visible structural response gives the touch a purpose. Vivid should make the lamp, letter and lightbox respond clearly at the moment an action is accepted. But Townscaper's freedom is a different promise from a short authored mystery: Mara needs an understandable question and a recognisable completed image. Neither format is inherently more fun; the human study must test the intended audience's preference.

## What this adds to the three design questions

**Why might someone play?** The directly observed loop combines intentional creation with surprises supplied by the building rules: joined roofs, railings and arches appeared from a few clicks. A plausible motivation is authorship without having to model every detail. Enjoyment itself still needs a human report.

**How could Vivid differ on a browser platform?** Offer a short authored question and a specific person or object to understand, while retaining the immediate visible response. Competing purely on pleasant miniature construction would place us close to this established experience.

**What unusually specific experience could Vivid deliver?** A physical mark that the player first treats as scenery later becomes evidence of a person's earlier act of care; their own final placement persists on return. That combination is a testable proposition, not proof of worldwide novelty. The demo's observed reset makes Vivid's memory promise especially important to verify through real reloads and visible recovery states.

## Actual endpoint

**Completed the bounded composition → revision → voluntary finish → exit/re-entry observation task.** The blue annex and pier were absent after re-entry; the initial red/yellow structure returned. This establishes only the observed default return behavior in this browser demo and session. It does not establish that saving is impossible, that a full edition loses progress, or that humans would enjoy this result. No game script errors were captured in this run; no performance measurement or physical-device compatibility test was conducted.
