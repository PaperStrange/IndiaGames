# Adventures With Anxiety! — observed player journey

**Status: one full story path completed through credits and the resource/Replay screen; real chapter resume and post-completion re-entry verified.** Exact edition: creator-hosted English HTML5 game at <https://ncase.me/anxiety/>. Date: 21 October 2026 UTC. Agent-operated visual play; see [method and limitations](README.md). Began visible UI interaction around 07:23 UTC; final re-entry observation was recorded at 08:20:34 UTC. The research browser was then closed. Input stopped temporarily at a late first-chapter choice screen while the team ran other browser checks. This pause is a research scheduling event, not a game failure.

| Discover / decide whether to start | Loaded the creator's page, pressed GO, then saw a title card advertising a 30-minute playtime and a content-notes option. | The displayed duration is the developer's claim; our session is not a timing validation. | Learn how to read | Used the visible reading controls and kept click-to-advance. The game explained that chapters auto-save. | This is an explicit pacing contract. | Learn the role | The game introduced the human, then assigned me the human's anxiety, represented by a red wolf. | The assigned role changes the meaning of identical dialogue buttons. ## Answers to the three design questions — hypotheses, not audience measurements

**Why someone might want to play:** this path offers an unusual perspective, recognizable concerns, callbacks to chosen details and a shift from conflict toward cooperation. The interest comes partly from discovering what the player's role means, not from pleasant events at every moment. The same fear warning feels different after the story has established distrust. Those are observed structures; whether they create interest, discomfort, identification or fatigue for a particular human audience still needs testing. The long reading sequence and repeated escalation are credible drop-off hypotheses, not measured boredom findings.

**How to differentiate on a browser platform:** make Vivid's physical object actions carry its meaning. Bracing Iona's lamp should enact care and persistence; aligning Mara's sketches should enact inference. The observed game changes the meaning of a familiar dialogue input as the relationship changes. Vivid can pursue that depth in shorter object interactions, with visible, understandable consequences. Preserve privacy/legacy accountability and give the return visit a quiet action rather than manufacturing another emergency. Replaceable characters should bring their own motives and action vocabulary; a generic chat menu with a renamed portrait would not reproduce this narrative effect.

**An unusually specific experience to offer:** return to a small illustrated place, physically arrange the object implicated in an earlier promise, and discover that the placement and response remember the player's decision. A second story can make understanding emerge from aligning a drawing instead of repeating the same rescue-and-choice pattern. The competitive proposal is this combination of compact authored stories, tactile meaning and restrained continuity. Memory callbacks alone are already present in this observed competitor. First-in-market uniqueness is unproven; compare remembered and neutral return conditions and ask players to explain the earlier action's connection to what they see.

## Limits and evidence reading

This was one agent-operated English browser path, with ordinary UI choices and assisted text advancement. It is not a blind human session or an enjoyment/retention result. The first two chapters ended in flight; the third reached the safe TIE outcome. The alternative bad ending, alternate first/second chapter outcomes, the other two optional epilogue fear discussions, all accessibility modes, physical touch devices and audio remain untested.

The creator interview was read after early play and disclosed that later chapters have alternative outcomes. No walkthrough or source code was used to select Anxiety answers. The late epilogue reading speed was explicitly set to its visible maximum with the settings slider and keyboard End. OCR sometimes missed colored dialogue panels; key screens were inspected visually. Some repetitive center clicks did not advance icon-only choices until visual inspection identified the controls. The session includes tool overhead, OCR stalls, a team scheduling pause and documentation work, so its elapsed wall time is not a player duration measurement.

Published context is kept separate from firsthand facts. [Storybench's Nicky Case interview](https://www.storybench.org/how-nicky-case-designed-the-game-adventures-with-anxiety/) explains the author's use of personally meaningful choices and small later callbacks within a largely shared plot. Our observed cat-GIF and flight callbacks are independent play evidence of that mechanism on this run. Neither source nor this run establishes clinical effectiveness, and the game itself distinguishes clicking story buttons from building a relationship with emotions.
