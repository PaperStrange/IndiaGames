# Adventures With Anxiety! — observed player journey

**Status: ongoing; first two chapter endings and real reload/resume verified; playing the third chapter toward its ending.** Exact edition: creator-hosted English HTML5 game at <https://ncase.me/anxiety/>. Date: 21 October 2026 UTC. Agent-operated visual play; see [method and limitations](README.md). Began visible UI interaction around 07:23 UTC. Input stopped temporarily at a late first-chapter choice screen while the team ran other browser checks. This pause is a research scheduling event, not a game failure.

## Journey so far

| Discover / decide whether to start | Loaded the creator's page, pressed GO, then saw a title card advertising a 30-minute playtime and a content-notes option. | The displayed duration is the developer's claim; our session is not a timing validation. | Learn the role | The game introduced the human, then assigned me the human's anxiety, represented by a red wolf. | The assigned role changes the meaning of identical dialogue buttons. It invites expression of a perspective rather than solving an objective quiz. This is an interpretation of the observed framing. |
| First meaningful choice | From three sandwich-related warnings I selected eating alone. The wolf escalated loneliness into a threat; a battle presentation labelled it fear of being unloved, with visible reduction in the human's energy bar. | There is immediate, understandable causality. The game character's health assertions are narrative text, not medical facts validated by this research. |
| First commitment | When a party invitation appeared, I chose to tell the human to accept because of loneliness. The human agreed while objecting to the wolf's pressure. | Consent to an action and assent to the speaker are visibly different. | Personal concern / consequence | I selected concern that a dating-app match was using the human. | Recognition / reversal | The human explicitly repeated the selected loneliness and cat-milk fears, then objected to the wolf's control. | First chapter ending | Reduced the human energy bar to zero, then chose flight rather than attacking the phone. This is an observed narrative contradiction, not a player enjoyment finding. |

## What this suggests for Vivid — hypotheses, not proven audience facts

**Why someone might want to play:** the opening offers an unusual perspective, clear fictional stakes and personally selectable concerns. Our observed loop is more specific than a general claim that people like emotional games. A human study must still determine whether this perspective is engaging, uncomfortable, familiar or tiring for a given audience.

**How to differentiate:** on a browser platform already full of choice stories, Vivid's physical object actions should carry the narrative meaning. Bracing Iona's lamp should enact care and persistence; aligning Mara's sketches should enact inference. Borrowing only dialogue menus would miss what made this example distinctive: its role makes the input emotionally specific.

**A candidate unique experience:** on return, place the exact object implicated in an earlier promise and see a restrained, authored acknowledgment of that choice. This is a testable proposed experience, not a proven first-in-market feature. Compare remembered and neutral return conditions; do not ask players merely whether they liked being remembered.
