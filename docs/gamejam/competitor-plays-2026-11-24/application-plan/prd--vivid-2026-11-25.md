# PRD — Vivid: one intervention that changes the portrait

**Status:** proposal, 2026-11-25. Nothing here is a solution until its gate passes.
**Order in the plan:** second. The synthesis's sequence runs the Vivid bounded comparison after the Notebook matrix (step 4).

## Description

Vivid turns a portrait into a responsive 2.5D place. Version 0.1.4 is frozen and staged; version 0.2.0 exists in source but was never played — its `dist/` is gitignored, its entry point uses a bare `three` import that needs the vite build, and playing it requires a preview-branch push. The baseline for this PRD is 0.1.4.

## Problem

The current experience is read-and-reveal: the portrait does not answer the player's intervention. No evidence shows whether a responsive portrait changes what the player does.

## Why now

Townscaper and Sandspiel supply the bounded comparison design — one intervention, a visible consequence, a recoverable revision — and the synthesis names it as the next step after the Notebook matrix. The comparison is bounded by design: one intervention, not a redesign.

## Success

A bounded comparison is run — one intervention visibly affecting a person or the world, with a recoverable revision — and it returns a recorded answer to whether the player notices the change, explains it, and chooses a second purposeful edit.

## Audience

The Vivid game agent. The comparison's subjects are fresh uncoached players.

## What

**H1 — the intervention changes a situation.** The team believes moving or removing one frame element will change a visible character situation, and the player will notice and explain the change. Evidence: Townscaper, where removing the lower storey produced a stilt cabin [REAL, `docs/competitor-learning-2026-11-25/records/townscaper.md`]; Sandspiel, where opening the drain and putting fire then water on a plank changed the basin [REAL, `docs/competitor-learning-2026-11-25/records/sandspiel.md`]. Gate: in the bounded comparison, the player describes the changed situation without prompting and makes a second purposeful edit. Kill: if players treat the change as decoration and make no second edit, the intervention is dropped.

**H2 — the trace is acknowledged on return.** The team believes the intervention will leave a consequential trace acknowledged on return. Evidence: the synthesis's distinctive-experience contract for Vivid — "the intervention leaves a consequential trace acknowledged on return" [SECONDARY — synthesis, not a play record, `docs/competitor-learning-2026-11-25/SYNTHESIS.md`]. Gate: on return, the player recognizes the trace and refers to their earlier edit. Kill: if no player recognizes the trace, the persistence is dropped.

**H3 — retention.** The team believes a new intervention decision each session will bring players back. Evidence: Codenames Daily (daily set, percentile, countdown) [REAL, `records/session--codenames-agent-2026-11-24.md`]; 2048 (single-number chase) [REAL, `records/session--2048-agent-2026-11-24.md`]. Gate: a player returns for a second session without prompting. Kill: no second-session return in the comparison window.

**Baseline note:** the comparison's baseline is 0.1.4 unless 0.2.0 is deployed and played first. This PRD does not assume 0.2.0's behavior.
