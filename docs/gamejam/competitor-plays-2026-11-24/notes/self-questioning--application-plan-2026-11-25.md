# Self-questioning — application plan (2026-11-25)

Solo adversarial pass, written before the plan exists. Each question names what it does to the plan. The plan's PRDs answer these by construction; a question the PRDs cannot answer stays open in the plan, not hidden.

## What would a skeptic ask

**1. Both deeps are agent sessions by one operator. Why should any application rest on them?**
The two deep comparisons (Counterfoil, Jigsaw Together) were played by one operator through the agent browser. They prove the games are completable and show what their loops do — they do not prove a human pair would experience them that way. Every PRD hypothesis is therefore written for a human gate, and no hypothesis counts as passed on agent evidence. More agent play cannot validate these applications.

**2. Jigsaw's "two players" was one person in two tabs. Is the shared-board model validated at all?**
No. The room held two live seats and the puzzle was solved end to end, but one operator drove both. The shared-board model is a shape observed in the room, not a validated pair experience. The Small Hours PRD states this limit on H1 and gates it on the six-pair test.

**3. Counterfoil has a solo mode. Does that break the "must be two people" claim for Small Hours?**
Partly. The shared-story model works solo — Counterfoil's "Both halves (solo)" header is capture-verified. So "shared story" does not by itself require two people. Small Hours H1 does not claim shared story requires two people; it claims the keepsake must visibly depend on both contributions. If the keepsake can be produced solo, H1's kill criterion fires.

**4. The keepsake differentiator is unproven. Does the plan assume it anyway?**
No. The scan's original claim ("no competitor makes a keepsake the pair owns") was corrected on 2026-11-25 after the remote audit: Gartic's shareable album is the nearest rival artifact. The Small Hours PRD tests the differentiator (H1, H2) rather than asserting it.

**5. Gartic's album and the keepsake are both auto-generated from play. Is the difference real?**
The claimed difference is authorship: Gartic's album is assembled by the system from player-made drawings; Small Hours' keepsake is assembled by the system from player-chosen text. Both are system-assembled. The PRD's H2 (the keepsake records a decision) is the test of whether that difference matters to a pair. If pairs treat the keepsake as a souvenir like the album, H2's kill criterion fires.

**6. Daily loops work for single-player score games. Do they transfer to two-person shared-memory games?**
Unproven. Codenames Daily and 2048 are single-player; a daily Small Hours prompt needs both people present at the same time, which is a coordination cost the score games do not have. The retention hypotheses name this as the likely kill cause if they fail.

**7. Bell's advance rule was verified in Contrast only. Does the plan assume it generalizes?**
No. Original mode, JA/zh journeys, and rules 3/4/5/7 are untested. The Bell PRD writes H1–H3 for Contrast and names Original mode and JA/zh as separate test surfaces, not assumed covered.

**8. Vivid 0.2.0 was never played. What is the PRD's baseline?**
0.1.4 (frozen, staged). The Vivid PRD states this explicitly and does not assume 0.2.0's behavior. If 0.2.0 is deployed and played first, the baseline moves and the hypotheses are re-checked against it.

**9. "Show the consequence before committing" — is that a decision change or a gesture?**
It fails the thesis filter unless the preview adds a real alternative. A preview that only informs is a gesture. The Bell PRD's H3 therefore requires the choice point to offer a genuine alternative (for example, declining the borrow) — if the preview informs but the choice set is unchanged, H3's kill criterion fires.

**10. TN-12 is incomplete. Can the Notebook experiments be selected now?**
No. The Notebook PRD makes TN-12 completion (Line Rider, Universal Paperclips including the next-day return) and the eight-family matrix a precondition, not a hypothesis. If the matrix changes the family map, the experiments are re-selected, not defended.

**11. No competitor with a two-person shared-memory keepsake was found. Is that absence meaningful?**
Only weakly. The scan covered 16 titles; the market is larger. "No competitor does this" was already rejected by the remote audit for the Nov-17 bundles on six titles. The plan never claims the keepsake is unique — it claims the differentiator is unproven and must be tested.

**12. Is "change the decision, not just the wording or gesture" itself validated?**
It is the governing filter from the synthesis (docs/competitor-learning-2026-11-25/SYNTHESIS.md), not a play finding. The PRDs apply it as the filter. The hypotheses test the applications, not the filter. If a hypothesis's gate fails, the application is killed — the filter stands.

## What is still unknown (carried into the PRDs, not resolved here)

- Whether any human pair values a shared keepsake at all (six-pair gate, empty).
- Whether a daily loop survives the two-person coordination cost.
- What Vivid 0.2.0 actually does (unplayed).
- Whether Bell's advance rule generalizes beyond Contrast.
- Whether the eight-family matrix, once populated, changes the Notebook experiment selection.
