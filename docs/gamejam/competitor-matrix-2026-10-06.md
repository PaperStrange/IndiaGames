# Six competitors per playable demo

**Revised 6 October 2026 · planning only · no new gameplay or game-code changes.** This matrix is the required coverage list for the [revised research plan](research-plan-2026-10-06.md). It replaces the earlier shared shortlist as the basis for answering the three product questions.

Each demo has **six required competitive alternatives and two reserves**. “Required” means a research commitment once the plan is agreed; licenses, devices, accounts and human participants remain conditional. Textual descriptions and platform claims below are source-attributed selection evidence, not firsthand game results. No title becomes “played” because its listing was read.

## Counting and relevance rules

- Six **distinct titles per demo**, spanning at least four broad play families, with at least two outside narrative/deduction puzzles and no more than two from one family. The family describes the dominant activity; two differently themed puzzle games do not automatically supply two genres.
- **Close** means a plausible substitute for a similar player experience or occasion. **Adjacent** shares a concrete desire but changes the format or context. **Occasion** competes for the same kind of leisure break; at most one counts toward each required six. These relationships are positioning hypotheses to test, not measured switching behavior.
- A native title may be a close experience comparison while remaining outside the browser platform's direct acquisition competition. Access/platform is always stated. A pure visual reference does not count toward the six.
- Shared titles are studied once where the evidence suffices, with a distinct question per demo. Replays, modes and ports do not increase the competitor count. The [coverage template](competitor-coverage-template.md) records this explicitly.
- A blocked, unplayed or listing-only entry does not satisfy the later firsthand coverage gate. A named reserve must preserve competitive relevance and at least four broad families; record any original question it leaves unanswered. Otherwise retain the gap and revise the roster. No automatic purchases, recruitment or account creation follow from this list.

All counts above describe the **selection**, not completed research. Browser listing/creator-link verification is not runtime verification. The broader plan requires several research sessions, access checks and actual people; it cannot promise all seventeen unique games as completed whole-game reviews in one day.

The previous twelve-browser-title pool and two partial carryovers remain available in the [earlier roster](candidate-roster-2026-10-06.md), but adding one to a demo requires a stated competitive reason and a new coverage count. Preserve the six-per-demo and four-family constraints after any substitution; the arithmetic of separate possible swaps does not guarantee that all swaps together work.

For the final three decision briefs, use the [observation templates index](observation-templates.md). Each recommendation must identify why someone might choose the competing experience, which cost or benefit our demo changes, and what observed result would make us abandon that proposed difference. No source-page claim alone settles whether a game is fun.
