## The experience, in order
The title screen set the hook before I pressed anything: a three-minute overture, and at the end of it she is dead, and then it runs again. I am the prompter. Nothing survives the reset but what I work out. Chilling, and I went in.

Loop 1 — the tourist. I landed in the prompt box, a tiny lit room at the bottom of a 3x3 grid of theater rooms: flies, grid, wardrobe, left wing, stage, green room, trap room, my hutch, prop store. I wandered with the arrow keys — the stage ("curtain down"), the wardrobe where I saw a woman in a yellow gown and a man in blue, the green room with its samovar, the rope gallery up in the flies. Facts trickled into the casebook and verbs unlocked one by one: FOLLOW, BOLT, DOUSE, CUE, PALM. By the end I had 10 of 12. I tried to palm the knife in the prop store but I was too slow — the game told me there was nothing there to take. The loop ended at bar 90 and the curtain screen told me she'd died under a sandbag, in front of nine hundred people. I felt flat. I'd spent three minutes watching. I hadn't DONE anything.

Loop 2 — the clever plan. This time I sprinted straight to the prop store and palmed the knife at bar 7: "THE KNIFE IS IN YOUR COAT." I felt brilliant. I staked out the flies, followed MARCH the flyman around, and then the house log delivered the line I'll never forget: "VELLO searches the prop store for something that is not there." I actually gasped — it worked, he was patting empty shelves while the knife sat in my coat! I was already celebrating. Then bar 90 came, and the curtain fell on the exact same death. She died anyway. I sat there stunned. My perfect plan meant nothing. That was the rug-pull, and it genuinely stung — the game let me feel smart just long enough to enjoy it, then took it away.

Loop 3 — listening for the beat. I sat in my box and reread the verbs. CUE: from the box, whisper a line to whoever is on the boards. She takes her mark at bar 88. I waited — and waiting in that little lit box while the bars ticked was the tensest part of the whole session — and pressed C at bar 84. "TOO EARLY, OR TOO LATE. THE LINE HAS TO LAND ON THE BEAT." A timing mechanic! I hadn't expected that at all. I waited, heart actually pounding, and pressed C again right at bar 88. It landed. She took the line downstage, off the mark — not at centre stage where the sandbag was waiting. The bag came down on bare boards. And then the screen just said: "SHE SINGS." She didn't even stop singing. I felt a real rush — after two loops of failure, I had actually, physically saved her by whispering at the right instant. The game even noted I was half a bar early, and it still counted. Mercy and precision at once.

Loop 4 — I went back to my box to cue her again, but I got distracted and missed the moment. She died. The curtain fell. I felt the loss more sharply this time, knowing I could have stopped it.

Loop 5 — just started when I decided I'd seen what I came for.

## The beats as I felt them
- The lie that got me: the knife. I was SO sure palming it would save her. The game even staged VELLO's frustrated search to make me feel clever — then killed her anyway. I never felt more outsmarted.
- The funniest moment: that same search. The image of VELLO patting empty shelves in the prop store while I sat in my hutch with his knife in my coat made me laugh out loud, alone at my desk.
- When I realized what the game was doing: at the end of loop 2. It wasn't about the weapon at all. I had to change HER — her mark, her timing — not the murderer's tools.
- How the loops felt on replay: loop 1 curious but passive; loop 2 active then heartbreaking; loop 3 tense then triumphant; loop 4 quietly sad. The three-minute wait is the hard part — sitting in a dark box doing nothing for a minute straight tests your patience. But it also makes the final bars electric.

One honest negative: the middle of each loop drags. Once you know the map, there's a long stretch where nothing happens and you're just killing time until bar 74. I caught myself pressing J open and closed just to have something to do. The game earns its ending, but it makes you wait for it.

## What kind of fun this was
It's the fun of a puzzle box that fights back — not laugh-out-loud funny, but the deep, quiet satisfaction of trying something, failing, feeling foolish, and then finding the one thread that unravels the whole thing. It's a tragedy you can interrupt, but only if you're listening for the beat instead of grabbing at the props. I felt clever, then foolish, then clever again, and the "SHE SINGS" moment — two words on a black screen after nine minutes of failure — was worth every loop.
