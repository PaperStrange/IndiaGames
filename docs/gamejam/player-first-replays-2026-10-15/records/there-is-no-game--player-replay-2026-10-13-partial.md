## The playthrough, in order
The game opens with a fake loading bar labeled "NOT LOADING," which got a smirk out of me — the game is already lying to me and I haven't even started. Then "NOT A GAME BY KAMIZOTO" and the big pixel title: THERE IS NO GAME. I clicked, and the narrator started in: he insists this has nothing to do with a game, and at one point tells me I can quit and leave him alone. I felt that little spark of mischief — oh, I'm definitely not leaving.

I started clicking the title letters like the disobedient player the game wants me to be. They wobble when you poke them. I kept hammering the O in "NO" — it lit up, rattled, dropped below the line, and hung there half-detached. It took a bunch of clicks (honestly more than felt good — I started wondering if my clicks were even landing), but when the O finally broke free and tumbled to the bottom of the screen, the narrator shouted "YOU'VE JUST RUINED THE TITLE!" I laughed out loud. There's something deeply satisfying about breaking a thing a narrator loves.

Then: "PUT IT BACK! PUT THE LETTER BACK!" He's properly panicking now. Of course I didn't put it back — I dragged the poor O all around the screen first, up to the corners, into the other letters. It kept sliding back down to the bottom like it was tired of me. When I finally dragged it up into the gap between the N and the G and it snapped home, the title restored itself and the narrator said, "BEAUTIFUL! ... NOW, PLEASE LEAVE. AND NEVER COME BACK." That line genuinely landed — the whiplash from gratitude to instant dismissal is the funniest beat in the whole sequence. I felt smug and a little stung at the same time.

And then the game broke. Right after that line, the screen went dead — the canvas vanished and the page fell back to a "browser does not support HTML5" message. I reloaded three times to try to continue; each time the game died sooner (once right after the "better with audio" screen). I sat with it a while, closed and reopened the whole browser — same thing. As a player, that arc from delight to "is this a gag or is it actually broken?" to plain frustration was real. The first 15 minutes were a joy; the last 20 were me fighting the renderer instead of playing.

## What kind of fun this was
This is the fun of being the villain in someone else's tidy little world — poking the smug narrator until he loses his composure, breaking his title letter by letter, and getting yelled at for it. The laughs come from his reactions, not from puzzles; every click is really just me asking "what will he say if I do THIS?" It's short, silly, mean-in-a-loving-way fun — like pulling the wings off a fly that talks back. I only wish my session hadn't ended on a technical crash instead of the goat.
