## The playthrough, in order
Pressed PLAY on the title screen: a warm, hand-drawn-looking kitchen, six cats in the title art, a little white ghost hovering by a teapot. Then the opening: a big, kind ghost grandmother appears in the kitchen and talks me through how she fell and how clumsy she feels now, and how her stomach rumbled when she got up and floated across the room to a cat. She asks me to get a cat into the chair, and says if I catch another, to drop it on the couch in the living room. She tells me the cat in her arms is her only green-eyed cat — all her others have blue eyes — and then the turn comes: she can only see a few of her cats today, and asks me to find the rest and bring them back to her. It landed gently — a small, domestic kind of heartbreak, not a horror setup.

Then the little white ghost — Grandma's ghost, it turns out — is mine to guide. I clicked around the kitchen and she floated to wherever I pointed, which felt immediately right: she's a small, determined ghost drifting through her own kitchen. Clicking the white cat by the armchair introduced me to Mr Spock, and the ghost remembered him tearing up her new curtains last week — that one made me smile, because of course he did. The cat asleep on top of the fridge is Fredrick, a very sleepy boy. The pink cat on the counter just sat there, unbothered. The plant on the shelf turned out to be catnip, and the ghost noted some of her cats go wild for it. The top shelf held an unopened bag of fish food, and she admitted her ghostly fingernails aren't sharp enough to rip it open — a lovely, sad little detail about how limited she is now.

The puzzle started to show itself when I clicked the door: it's the only way out of the kitchen, but her poltergeist powers aren't strong enough to open it, and she mused that she can usually only get "his" attention when tasty food is involved. I tried to build her strength the only ways I found: I turned on the little fan on the shelf and watched it spin, and I toppled the pile of boxes by the door, which made a mess she brushed off by saying she needed to find a way to help her precious kitties escape the apartment. That line reframed everything for me — she's not just tidying up; she's trying to get her cats OUT, and she can't do it alone. But the heavy things all refused her: the fishbowl wouldn't budge, the fridge and the frying pan were too heavy, and the sofa was too big for her to move even back when she was alive. The mug and utensils, she said, weren't going to help right now. I was about to try waking Fredrick when the tooling gave out.

## What kind of fun this was
The quiet, tender kind: not a challenge to beat but a small haunted house to sit in, learning a ghost's routines and limits, meeting one cat at a time and feeling how much she loves them and how little she can still do for them. Even stuck in one room, it felt cozy and a bit achy, like visiting someone who is doing their best with what they have left.
