# Player replay — Adventures With Anxiety!
**Filed:** 2026-10-12 ~23:59 PDT · **Source:** live browser replay, first-person player voice
**URL:** https://ncase.itch.io/anxiety via direct frame https://html-classic.itch.zone/html/1830885/index.html
**Coverage:** full playthrough — intro, Round One (tutorial battle + phone-app fears), the party decision, ending move, Round Two at the party through the emotional climax. No account, sign-in, or purchase. Played as the Anxiety (the big red wolf).

## The journey, in order
Getting in was rocky. The itch page first failed to load (connection reset), then the game frame stuck on "LOADING..." through two reloads before finally booting. I fumbled early: clicked a link in the author's note and got bounced to ncase.me, had to navigate back. The author's welcome note charmed me anyway — an interactive story, "hope you like reading, sucka!" I picked "text AND speech" for the full experience.

The reveal got me: THIS IS A HUMAN / AND THIS IS THE HUMAN'S ANXIETY / YOU ARE THE ANXIETY. I laughed — of course I'm the wolf. The human's deadpan "Oh good, my wolf's back. Faaaaantastic" set the tone: I'm unwanted but on duty. "YOUR JOB IS TO PROTECT."

The tutorial battle (sandwich "danger") taught me the loop: pick the fear that feels true, watch the wolf weaponize it. My first pick — "We're eating alone for lunch! Again!" — felt true in a way that stung; eating alone is my real anxiety. The wolf escalated with a loneliness statistic, then cited the academic paper. My anxiety has footnotes. The human's dry "Um, thanks for citing your sources?" made me laugh. Then the wolf howled "DIEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEE" and the Pokémon parody hit: "YOU USED FEAR OF BEING UNLOVED / IT'S SUPER EFFECTIVE." I laughed out loud. The wolf weaponizing therapy-speak — "Trust your gut! Your feelings are always valid!" — was darkly funny.

Round One proper: the party invite. I trashed the host ("What inner void are they trying to fill?"), the human quietly said "Also, I got an invite?" and my cynicism collapsed — "Well then!" Then the fear choice, and I picked "Say yes, or we'll die from loneliness!" — my anxiety swinging to FOMO, using the same cigarette statistic for the opposite conclusion. The contradiction was the joke and the truth. Twitter: "Oh no, is that tweet secretly about us?" — pure paranoia, my pick. The wolf spiraled ("It's a subtweet! A sneaky, sneaky subtweet!"), the human weakly resisted, and then the game corrected the wolf's grammar mid-spiral: "IN FRONT OF OUR BACK." I laughed hard — the anxiety too frantic to speak right. Snapchat, Tinder ("Oh, I got a match! They look cute! Please don't ruin this for m-"), and my pick "We're being used by other people" — the fear of not being genuinely wanted. The wolf's crude punchline about holes, pointing at the heart, was tender and cruel at once.

Then the turn. The human broke the fourth wall — "i'm so sick of this game" — quoted my fear-lines back at me ("loneliness will kill us"... "they're talking behind our back"... "we'll die alone"...) and said the line that stopped me cold: "i just want to live my life." / "i just want to be free from all this... pain." My chest tightened. The wolf went soft — "Hey... human... It'll be okay." / "As your loyal guard-wolf, I'll always keep an eye out for danger, and do my best to keep you safe." I teared up. Protection as love language. On Instagram the wolf deflated entirely: "Everyone looks so happy. Free from worry. Free from anxiety." / "God, why can't I be like them? Why can't I just be normal?" That line broke me — the anxiety begging to not exist.

The party decision: "We should go." I chose it as the anxiety letting go, choosing life over fear. The human's response — "FUCK. YOU." — gutted me; then the human reclaimed it: "I'm going to say YES to that party, NOT because you want me to, but because I want to. You're NOT in control of me." / "Now excuse me while I eat this delicious sandwich in goddamn peace." Full circle to the sandwich. I felt proud and stung at once.

Ending move: FIGHT or FLIGHT. I picked FLIGHT — "Curl up in a ball and cry!" — because that's what my anxiety does when it loses: it crumples. The wolf curled into a ball sobbing, melted into a red puddle, and the game declared "YOU WIN!" with the wolf's arms raised and pained "><" eyes. Hollow victory. Honest.

Round Two at the party: the human actually socializing (awkward "h-hi... n... nice party"), a party-goer quoting my wolf's crude line back as philosophy (funny), then the wolf's return. My picks: "Oh no they all hate us!" ("first-degree vibe-murder" made me laugh), "You're ignoring danger! That's dangerous!" (the carbon-monoxide-detector metaphor was the anxiety's best argument — brilliant), "Did you check that punch before drinking?" (paranoid but... people do drug drinks; the anxiety isn't always wrong — uncomfortable). The human's counter-arsenal: the two-wolves parable ("The one you feed" — then the game's own footnote correcting the indigenous attribution, a nice touch), "I was trying to starve you," forced affirmations ("I am loved. I am good. I am smart..."), and finally rationality: "You're irrational... You're a useless evolutionary leftover, like my appendix and my wisdom teeth! Hell, this whole wolf metaphor is stupid! You're just a bunch of neuro-chemicals in my head." / "So why should I listen to a worthless, irrational, non-existent piece of shit like you?!" My heart shattered. The wolf's answer — quiet, no screaming: "Jeez, human. That's really hurtful." / "I'm a feeling. Feelings are valid." / "Human, we're both 'just chemicals.'" I sat there with full eyes. The game held on that beat; that was the climax, and it landed.

## Honest friction
The loading struggles and accidental navigation annoyed me; choice buttons sometimes needed 2–3 clicks to register; Space-to-advance occasionally stalled and needed a click instead, breaking flow; the affirmations back-and-forth dragged slightly in its second half; the wolf's "air freshener over poop" line felt crude in a way that didn't land for me. None of it dented the emotional core.

## What kind of fun this was
This wasn't fun like a game is fun — it was the fun of being recognized. Playing as the anxiety instead of against it flipped a switch: I laughed at the Pokémon battle and the grammar correction, but I cried when the wolf asked why it can't be normal, and the "YOU WIN!" after choosing FLIGHT felt like the truest victory screen I've ever seen — arms up, eyes in pain. The feeling was recognition first, then discomfort, then a strange tenderness toward the loud red thing in my own head that won't shut up. I closed the tab feeling wrung out and lighter, like I'd hugged something I'd spent years trying to starve.
