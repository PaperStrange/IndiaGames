## The run, in order
The title screen's warning made me laugh — it promised snobbery, rudeness, and mass murder in one breath, which felt absurd for a game about little shape-people. Then a quote card landed hard: "We become what we behold. We shape our tools and then our tools shape us." with a wry "(misattributed)" that made me trust the game's sense of humor.

The tutorial dropped me into a field of circle- and square-headed peeps milling around a TV. My first photo caught a plain square peep — captioned "just a normal peep" — and I felt properly told off for aiming at the boring one. The one interesting person was a little guy in a bowler hat, and chasing him was genuinely frustrating: the camera lags behind the cursor and he kept wandering, hiding behind the TV, drifting to the edge of the frame. When I finally caught him, the photo caught just his chin and tie, captioned "#OOH NICE HAT", and that felt like a small victory.

Then the broadcast: the crowd gathered around the TV, watched my photo, and when I looked again, six of them were wearing bowler hats, grinning. That was the moment the game clicked for me — I had done that, just by pointing. It felt like a magic trick, and a little creepy. Act 1 began, and I photographed two hat-wearers — a circle and a square, face to face, both in bowlers — captioned "#HAT PARTY!!". The crowd was gathering around the TV to watch that broadcast when the screenshot view died.

What blocked me: from that moment on, every screenshot came back empty, on both the game frame and the itch page. I tried reloading, navigating back and forth, waiting, retrying — none restored the view. Visual click/hover control still works, but it cannot show me the game, and playing a canvas game blind would mean inventing headlines, crowd reactions, and an ending I never saw. So I stopped honestly rather than faking Acts 1–3.

## What kind of fun this was
The fun of pointing at something and watching a whole crowd copy it — silly and a bit eerie at the same time, like being handed a toy megaphone and realizing everyone actually listens. I wanted to keep going, and I'm annoyed the tooling cut the session short just as the hats were spreading.
