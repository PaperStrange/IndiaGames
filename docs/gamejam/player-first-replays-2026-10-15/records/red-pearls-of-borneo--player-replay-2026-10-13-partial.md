## The playthrough, in order
I pressed a key, picked English, and hit a content warning about period-typical racial insults against the Japanese army. I chose to continue uncensored — I wanted the text as written. Then the game opened on a study in December 1941, heavy teak furniture and stale smoke, and Jeremiah Goodwill picking up a rotary telephone. He's a colonial plantation owner, pompous and suspicious, logging an unexpected visitor with the district officer: a young man named Lucas Sterling who washed up by raft claiming to be the sole survivor of a pirate attack on the coast. Jeremiah doesn't buy it. He calls the kid a young pup, too cheeky, maybe a common scoundrel. His head guard Kanan already interrogated him at the boathouse. Then someone knocks, and a voice says: "Tuan Bone is waiting for you in the Library." That line hooked me. I wanted to go to the Library immediately.

And then I got stuck. I opened the island map — a gorgeous little painted plantation, main house, outbuildings, a jetty — and clicked the main house over and over. Nothing happened. I hovered, I clicked the "Study" hotspot, and it just replayed the intro. I felt genuinely lost, like I'd missed a door. The game had told me where to go and I couldn't get there. That frustration is real and it lasted a while.

What saved me was poking around the UI. The Guests panel opened a case file: Jeremiah and Lune Goodwill already identified, nine more silhouettes waiting with IDENTIFY buttons. Eleven guests. That got my detective brain going. Then I found the Evidence Cabinet and read the Help, which explained the loop: select guests, focus on them, unlock echoes; items work the same way for partial echoes; the Mind's Eye tracks everyone across time; you can take notes on anything by clicking it.

The Mind's Eye was the first thing that made me feel like a detective. I clicked the 4pm slot and saw the Study holding three people: Jeremiah, Lune, and an UNKNOWN. Someone else was in that room. Who?

Then I started clicking words. Clicking the blue name "Lucas Sterling" in the dialogue opened an investigation-note panel, and I wrote down what I'd learned: young man, raft, pirate story, Jeremiah distrusts him, interrogated at the boathouse. Saving that note felt like real detective work — my own words, in my own file. Clicking the purple "Rotary Telephone" gave me a flash: NEW ITEM DISCOVERED. That little hit of discovery felt great.

Then Delphine spoke. Delphine is me, in a way — the clairvoyant investigator in 1948 London, sensing echoes. Her hint was that since Jeremiah owns the plantation, he's probably the one who uses the telephone. So I went into the cabinet, opened Items, clicked the phone, hit IDENTIFY OWNER, and picked Jeremiah. The phone lit up green: Aura Infused. And Delphine came back with the line that made my whole session: the telephone has strong energy, and she can feel it being used at 2am by a man with a Bornean accent. Not Jeremiah. Someone else. At 2am. I sat there thinking — who is on the phone at 2am, and why? That was the moment the game clicked for me. I had a thread to pull.

I tried to chase it — opened the Study timeline, hunted for the 2am slot — and got stuck again on the interface. The entry was scrolled out of view, the cabinet wouldn't close when I clicked its button (I eventually learned the 'i' key toggles it), and I never did see the 2am call. I ended the session in the Study with that lead burning a hole in my pocket.

## What kind of fun this was
Slow-burn, notebook-and-corkboard fun — the kind where you stare at a timeline, mutter "who was where at 2am," and feel a genuine little jolt when a hunch pays off. The first hour is fiddly and the interface fights you sometimes, and I got lost and frustrated more than once. But the moment Delphine sensed that 2am phone call, I was hooked — it felt like the case had just opened its first real door, and I wanted to keep pulling the thread.
