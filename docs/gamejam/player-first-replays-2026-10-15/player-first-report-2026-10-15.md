# What It Felt Like to Play Them — a player-first report
**13 games, played as a player · fresh live-browser sessions, Oct 12–15, 2026**

## How this was made (short version)

I played every slot as a player, not a reviewer — fresh sessions, no walkthroughs, no hints, no guides. What follows is what surprised me, what made me laugh, what moved or frustrated me, and exactly how far I got in each game. Where a session ended early, I say why, and I'm careful about the difference between "my tools broke" and "the game dragged." Nothing is faked: unfinished games are bounded, guesses are labeled, and one slot (The Roottrees are Dead, two failed attempts, zero deductions) was filled by a verified replacement, Demonology 101 — the failure is footnoted, not hidden. Most screenshots died in capture; the few survivors (WBWWB ×3, Demonology 101 ×1) are staged with the records and appear in the hosted version of this report.

## Full plays

### The Temple of No — "a joke factory with a heart it refuses to admit to"
**How far:** full playthrough, FROG branch, one ending.

It starts by scolding you for clicking, which is the whole relationship in miniature: the game pretends not to care about you and then keeps careful track of everything you do. It asked what scares me and I answered honestly — a storm — only to be shut down cold: "Enough of that. This isn't about YOU." I laughed a startled laugh. I refused Mrs Watts twice and felt genuinely guilty about it — the game remembered that too. The forced singalong is funny precisely because it's forced; the stairs and the snake-room ellipsis drag on a replay, and I'll admit I checked my patience there. But the vow — the silly little vow it makes you take — becomes the spine of the thing, and at the "Kill him" moment it lands like something sincere. The narrator reveal (Old Green Guy, obviously, in retrospect) got me. Comedy that keeps insisting it's not serious, until it is.

### Adventures With Anxiety! — "like I'd hugged something I'd spent years trying to starve"
**How far:** full playthrough.

The reveal — YOU ARE THE ANXIETY — made me laugh out loud, and then the game spent the rest of its runtime making that joke ache. The Pokémon-battle catastrophizing is funny; the wolf quoting my own fear-lines back at me verbatim at the climax was not funny at all, in the best way. The wolf asking why it can't be normal — I teared up, and I'm writing that down because it's true. Choosing FLIGHT and getting "YOU WIN!" felt like the hollowest, truest victory screen I've ever seen. Friction, honestly: the loading struggled, some choices took two or three clicks to register, and the affirmations section dragged. But the core loop — recognition, discomfort, humor, then tenderness toward the thing — is the reason to play.

### PROMPTBOX — "a tragedy you can interrupt, but only if you're listening for the beat"
**How far:** four full loops plus a fifth started; casebook 10/12.

Loop 1 I played passive and she died and I felt flat — the game punishing my detachment. Loop 2 I got clever: palmed the knife at bar 7, watched VELLO search the empty shelf ("I actually gasped") — and then she died anyway. The rug-pull. I have never felt more outsmarted by a game; it let me feel brilliant for exactly long enough. Loop 3 I found the real mechanic hiding underneath: not grabbing props, but timing — the CUE. I waited with my heart actually pounding and landed it at bar 88 and SHE SINGS, and it was worth every loop. Loop 4 I missed the moment and felt the loss harder than any of the deaths. Honest drag: the mid-loop waiting, killing time until the bars that matter. But it also makes the final bars electric.

## Deep partials

### A Dark Room — "tending something fragile in the dark"
**How far:** ~50 minutes; reached the trading post; stopped at the 400-fur compass with 3 fur in hand. The grind wall is the honest endpoint, not a failure.

The opening lines still land. The Ruined Trap filled me with dread before I understood the systems. I got genuinely fond of my little village — and then the beasts attacked and wiped the population from 4/4 to 0/4, and I felt that loss. Penrose and the dust gave me chills. And then the last fifteen minutes were pure gather-clicking: wood, fur, wood, fur. Boring. Both things are true — the first half is one of the most affecting stretches in this whole set, and the back half is an idle-game spreadsheet, and that's where I stopped.

### We Become What We Behold — "I made that happen"
**How far:** intro, tutorial, and the hat fad complete; the Act-3 grab wall beaten on my 9th attempt ("# CRAZED SQUARE ATTACKS"); the scared-crowd escalation seen. The ending unplayed — the page started scrolling away after every click and the session died. (An earlier pass had died mid-Act-1 when screenshots failed.)

This game roasts you and it's the tutorial. "WOWWEE, IT'S NOTHING" in rainbow letters for photographing empty space; "just a normal peep" for a boring shot. The "(misattributed)" McLuhan gag made me grin; the recursive TV photographing my photo delighted me. Then I photographed a hat and six characters wore bowler hats, and a minute later "# nvm hats aren't cool anymore" and they were gone — I created and killed a fad in sixty seconds and felt a pang, which is the entire point arriving early. The grab wall took nine real attempts, each miss taunted ("oooooh just missed it"), and catching it was a genuine rush. Then the crowd looked scared, and the rush curdled: my photo had turned a jerk into a news event. The news doesn't report reality here; it manufactures it, and it used my hands. A gut-punch disguised as a toy. I don't know the ending, and I'm not going to pretend the arc resolved — it ends for me on the scared crowd, which might be the truest place to stop anyway.

### Different Strokes — "it sold me on wanting to paint; it just never handed me the brush"
**How far:** 55 minutes; tutorial read in full, gallery walked extensively, art wall studied, two NPCs, rules board, settings. Never found a paintable easel; the painting UI never opened; the social loop never materialized. (Whether the missing easels were my tools or the game, I can't say — reporting it as experienced.)

The first twenty minutes were genuinely delightful. The tutorial NPC is an adorable ghost-bat kid and its "SUPER-SPECTACULAR COLLABORATORIUM" speech is so earnest it made me want to make something. The player art wall is the star: a pixel-art bee over a red flower in a baroque frame, an orange boxing-glove cat, a green frog-monster, a jack-o'-lantern demanding you smell its feet. I stood in front of the bee for a while. The plague-doctor bird's big spooky secret — "IT WAS THE EXIT" — made me laugh out loud. And then: fifteen minutes hunting for an easel that never appeared, E-prompts that wouldn't fire, and a gallery empty of other players, the whole "strangers finishing each other's art" premise hanging in the air unfulfilled. Charming world, warm writing, real soul in the art — but I bounced off the core loop because I couldn't find it.

### Sort the Court! — a soft-hearted king who is terrible with money
**How far:** ~Day 6 in the filed run. (A retry reportedly reached ~Day 12 before capture died again, but no player record of that run exists — the account below is the Day-6 run.)

You get handed a crown and immediately discover that every good instinct costs you something. Winston the slime got his anti-bullying decree. Chester ate folks anyway after two NOs, and I sat with the blood money guilt. The dragon's extortion collapsed from 403 gold to 3. The chicken paid rent in eggs. And the All-Seeing Eye — +100 gold one day, -100 the next — never trust a giant eyeball twice. That one stung because I'd started to trust the face. Yes/no has never felt this loaded. It ended mid-reign through no fault of the game; my tools lost the picture.

### The Red Pearls of Borneo — "the moment the game clicked"
**How far:** intro plus the first real deduction; the case unsolved, one lead unchased.

I was lost on the island map for a while — clicked the main house repeatedly while nothing happened, felt foolish, kept going. Then: the rotary phone attuned to Jeremiah Goodwill, and Delphine sensing it was used at 2am by a man with a Bornean accent. That was the moment the game clicked — the fantasy of being a detective. I filled in the game's own investigation-note panel, which felt like real detective work. The Mind's Eye 4pm slot showing an UNKNOWN third person in the Study gave me a proper chill. Honest notes: the interface confused me (I learned the 'i' key too late, a cabinet wouldn't close), and the itch comments below the game contained spoilers — I saw them, I didn't use them, and I'm saying so. The case is unsolved and it bothers me, which I take as a compliment to the game.

### Demonology 101 — "oh, OF COURSE" (replaces The Roottrees are Dead)
**How far:** 2 of 5 demons named (bbaddon, eergh); both confirms were triumphs; never mocked. Stopped by choice.

This slot belonged to The Roottrees are Dead, which got two full attempts and zero deductions — the tools locked me out at the family tree the first time, and before the page even loaded the second — and a proposed replacement (The Case of the Golden Idol's browser demo) turned out to no longer exist, a miss I own. Demonology 101 was verified live before I promised it, then played blind. It's a comedy logic-puzzle about learning demons' true names from a clue catalog, and the "click" of a correct name is a little jolt: for bbaddon, a pun snapping shut (four sugar-words in its speech, one catalog line — "can't stop talking about sugary things"); for eergh, a logic gate flipping (every sentence a lie, inverted through the "opposite of their sibling" rule until the truths spelled the page). The writing is the star — the catalog's author "Bill" praising his own humility, the demon insisting "MY NAME IS NOT BILL," "Obvious, captain of the void," Wikipedia contributor. Friction, honestly: the catalog flips one page at a time with no jump, so each confirm meant a dozen clicks, and the itch page kept dumping spoiler-filled comments into my view while scrolling (I didn't decode or use any — the reasoning above is all in-game evidence). Short, charming, and the demons have real personality.

## Fragments, walks, and parked games

### There Is No Game — "BEAUTIFUL! ... NOW, PLEASE LEAVE."
**How far:** the title-breaking opening, complete. The renderer died right after; a retry found the site crashing the browser service outright. The goat remains unmet — a tooling wall, not the game's.

The fake "NOT LOADING" bar. Knocking the O out of the title. "PUT IT BACK!" And then, after all that work, the whiplash dismissal: "BEAUTIFUL! ... NOW, PLEASE LEAVE." The whiplash from gratitude to instant dismissal is the funniest beat in the whole sequence — I felt smug and a little stung at the same time. The narrator's fury at being dismantled is the whole joke and it never misses. What's here is flawless; what's beyond it I can't report, and I won't invent.

### Detective: Again! — fragments of a case I know
**How far (replay):** fragments only — the victim's flirty briefing, the "adorable" exchange, Mark's genre roast, the "again" landing; on retries, the ghost with luggage, the time-machine cloche gasp, Mark's entrance, a rainy alley, doors A and B, a bathroom detour, the day bleeding into a white clock-and-arrows reset. Three attempts, all killed by the same tooling wall (dead renderer, dead clicks). The full solve — the red gun migrating to the man's hand, the threat that IS the confession — comes from the earlier 2026-10-06 session, not this replay, and I'm attributing it there.

What the fragments still gave me: the victim briefing is funnier than it has any right to be, Mark's entrance has real presence, and the loop reset genuinely unsettled me even knowing the structure. A game I like, glimpsed through a broken window.

### Make it Meme — "a room with no show until someone else walks in"
**How far:** a solo walk to the hard gate, and no further — honestly.

Lobby #113, invite code BIUXE, auto-named "ProSloth" (the funniest thing the session produced, courtesy of the game's own name generator). Picked packs, picked Topics mode, set 20 topics, tweaked settings — and "Let's play!" sat disabled: at least 2 players. The topic chips ("Why am I like this?", "Ok Boomer") suggest the humor; the empty lobby is the experience. No templates, no rounds, no players fabricated. This one can't be judged solo — the game is a room, and rooms need people. A lobby dressed for a party with no guests.

### Six Cats Under — parked in the kitchen (per Sakura's decision)
**How far:** opening plus real kitchen exploration — the ghost grandmother, Mr Spock's curtain-shredding crime, sleepy Fredrick on the fridge, the catnip, the fish-food bag she can't open, the door puzzle, "get her cats OUT." Then the tools died again. Sakura chose to park it rather than take over the browser, so the kitchen partial stands as the record.

What's here is warm: the title screen landed like a painting hung in a hallway, and the kitchen has genuine cozy-mystery texture — a grandmother's house where the cats are the point and the ghost is the help. What's beyond the kitchen is unknown, and I'm leaving it unknown rather than guessing.

## Moments that stayed with me

Not a ranking — just the beats I keep turning over: the Temple of No's vow at the "Kill him" moment. The anxiety wolf asking why it can't be normal. "SHE SINGS" at bar 88. The beast attack wiping my village to zero. Creating and killing a hat fad in sixty seconds. The scared crowd after my headline. The bee painting in the empty gallery. The "LIER" typo in a demon's dialogue. Being told "BEAUTIFUL! ... NOW, PLEASE LEAVE." None of these were inferred from design docs. They happened to me, in a browser, while playing.
