# Keeper’s Frame / Vivid — next-iteration review and plan

Review date: 2026-10-20. **Proposed iteration; no game feature changes or competitor launches.** This subreview performed no Git writes; the coordinator publishes the combined documentation separately. See the [dashboard](README.md) for shared plan and research gates.

## Recommendation

Test one short return encounter in which the player's private/public choice changes an object in the frame and one action Iona invites them to perform. Preserve the existing ferry rescue as the first encounter. The question is whether a player understands and values **“I chose this; she remembered; now I can do this.”** More portrait animation, photo reconstruction, and open conversation would not answer that question.

**Why play, hypothesis:** a short act of practical care becomes a relationship the player can revisit. The player sees what Iona cannot reach; she gives the observation a purpose. The visual frame offers curiosity, and the remembered promise may offer a reason to return.

**Difference from the comparison set:** the distinctive proposition is a bounded spatial encounter with an authored person, followed by a verifiable consequence of the player's treatment of her. Depth, remembered choices, and conversational characters individually have precedents. This combination is a product hypothesis, not established market uniqueness. The historical research's “nobody ships” language is too strong to carry into the pitch.

## Evidence that changes the plan

The revised Oct 15 player report and its records are useful bounded observations, not a human participant study. The team's [field-report evidence definitions](https://github.com/PaperStrange/IndiaGames/blob/0b35e3fb23bef222f3f3cae72d2b78f6ab72d18b/docs/gamejam/field-report.md#L9) explicitly separate agent play from enjoyment/retention evidence, and [human-playtest.csv](https://github.com/PaperStrange/IndiaGames/blob/0b35e3fb23bef222f3f3cae72d2b78f6ab72d18b/docs/gamejam/human-playtest.csv#L1) still contains only its header. “I cried,” “I laughed,” and similar first-person agent prose must not become player quotes or measured emotional outcomes.

- **Playable memory, with disagreement retained:** the [validated practice](https://github.com/PaperStrange/IndiaGames/blob/0b35e3fb23bef222f3f3cae72d2b78f6ab72d18b/docs/gamejam/player-first-replays-2026-10-15/design-insights-practices-validated-2026-10-15.md#L9) supports performing a remembered choice. The [contrast record](https://github.com/PaperStrange/IndiaGames/blob/0b35e3fb23bef222f3f3cae72d2b78f6ab72d18b/docs/gamejam/player-first-replays-2026-10-15/design-insights-contrast-2026-10-15.md#L26) retains the objection that unchanged outcomes can make callbacks decorative. That directly challenges the current repeated ferry/letter sequence.
- **Specific recall:** the [Anxiety record](https://github.com/PaperStrange/IndiaGames/blob/0b35e3fb23bef222f3f3cae72d2b78f6ab72d18b/docs/gamejam/player-first-replays-2026-10-15/records/adventures-with-anxiety--player-replay-2026-10-12.md#L15) records earlier choices being quoted back. Borrow the checkable structure of input becoming later response. The revised report states the emotional-climax capture did not survive; neither prose nor other screenshots establishes human emotion at that scene.
- **Manual consequence:** the [WBWWB retry record](https://github.com/PaperStrange/IndiaGames/blob/0b35e3fb23bef222f3f3cae72d2b78f6ab72d18b/docs/gamejam/player-first-replays-2026-10-15/records/we-become-what-we-behold--player-replay-2026-10-14-retry.md#L18) records a photo causing a visible hat fad, and later records crowd change. Its ending was not reached. Use the observable action-to-world-change pattern for placing the remembered object; do not import time-window difficulty or treat an agent's “guilt” as a design target.
- **Keep the supports already working:** safe rehearsal, an external evidence log, and retraceable reasoning are supported by the [practices](https://github.com/PaperStrange/IndiaGames/blob/0b35e3fb23bef222f3f3cae72d2b78f6ab72d18b/docs/gamejam/player-first-replays-2026-10-15/design-insights-practices-validated-2026-10-15.md#L12). There is no need to discard them for more elaborate dialogue.

Original Vivid research remains helpful as a design history: [self-QA](https://github.com/PaperStrange/IndiaGames/blob/0b35e3fb23bef222f3f3cae72d2b78f6ab72d18b/vivid-2.5d-photo-game/research/04-organized-findings-and-self-qa.md#L51) questioned depth versus responsiveness and whether live conversation was necessary; the [revised novelty thesis](https://github.com/PaperStrange/IndiaGames/blob/0b35e3fb23bef222f3f3cae72d2b78f6ab72d18b/vivid-2.5d-photo-game/research/08-revised-novelty-thesis.md#L26) foregrounds purpose, authored persona, separate adjudication, and memory. Its specific competitive coverage and provider claims are dated, qualified research, not newly verified facts. ## One bounded signature experiment: “A place for the promise”

**Proposed, not implemented.** Keep one portrait and the existing first rescue. Replace the repeat-ferry return with approximately one minute of new authored interaction; that duration is a content target, not a human timing result.

| Previous choice | New visible state on return | Invited action and consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keep the letter private | Iona holds the folded letter toward a sheltered place inside the frame. | The player leans, then places it in the private recess with her invitation. It remains there on reload; Iona acknowledges exactly what the player protected. |
| Add it to the harbour record | An approved dedication awaits placement on the harbour-facing edge of the frame. | The player leans, then pins the dedication where the harbour can see it. The dedication remains on reload; Iona acknowledges exactly what the player chose to share. |

Both paths have equivalent interaction count and care. Prior uninvited reading remains truthfully acknowledged; later permission does not erase it. The return reaches a quiet stopping point without replaying the unchanged ferry puzzle. No second portrait, new currency, free-text input, or full narrative chapter is included.

**Test design:** six fresh human sessions for the enacted-memory condition and, if recruitment permits, six different participants for a neutral return of comparable length and interaction count. The neutral return offers a routine frame task without referring to the prior choice. Keep the first rescue, art quality, controls, and exposure length comparable. This comparison tests the complete causal-memory return package; it cannot isolate the separate effect of the line, object, and permission.

Before any request to revisit, record whether a participant chooses to return. Then standardize exposure by asking remaining participants to open the return scene, and label these returns prompted. Ask neutral questions such as “What changed?” and “What do you think caused that?” Record actual N, help, time, verbatim consented response, branch, and device. Do not seed “memory,” “trust,” or “permission” in the question. Keep spontaneous and prompted discovery separate.

**Proposed diagnostic gate, not statistical proof:** at least 4/6 in the enacted-memory condition complete without help and explain the causal connection between their own choice and the new action; no recurring false expectation of unrestricted AI chat or real-photo animation. If both conditions are understood equally but only appearance is recalled, strengthen or reconsider the relationship consequence before adding content. If recruitment is below six, report observations without calling the gate passed. Return intent is directional; same-session clicking is not day-two retention. A genuine later revisit requires a later measurement.

## Defer

Photo uploads, depth inference, real-time avatar providers, LLM/voice dialogue, extra portraits, monetization, daily rewards, and a full second puzzle remain outside this iteration. The missing evidence is whether a remembered choice creates a reason to act again. Keep browser-local memory and original art for this test. Broader browser/device coverage can follow the pilot's actual audience needs; screen-reader and physical-phone results must not be inferred from Chromium emulation.
