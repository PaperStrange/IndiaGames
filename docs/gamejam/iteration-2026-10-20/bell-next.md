## The design gap to resolve

**Forecasts currently describe a schedule; the encounters do not enact that schedule.** Scene selection uses `worldIndex = loop - 1`, then `worlds[world][district]`; phase changes the label but does not select a different encounter. Forecast answers use phase alone. The full people×phase table is visible from the beginning. [World selection](https://github.com/PaperStrange/IndiaGames/blob/1be02bc9fc0b846e4c24cb1ceb67ddd69ebfe590/js/engine.js#L15), [visit selection](https://github.com/PaperStrange/IndiaGames/blob/1be02bc9fc0b846e4c24cb1ceb67ddd69ebfe590/js/engine.js#L86), [ledger and authored forecasts](https://github.com/PaperStrange/IndiaGames/blob/1be02bc9fc0b846e4c24cb1ceb67ddd69ebfe590/js/data.js#L39).

[Captured mismatch](evidence/bell/baseline-noon-forecast-scene.png). The same source selection applies to Contrast. This is a game-design consistency problem, not a crash. A “Witnessed” label presently means a boundary reveal was displayed, even if the player never visited that district.

The minute is mechanically real, but why it matters to a person remains largely prose. ## Proposed experiment: one minute changes Mina's day

**Why play:** a short solo mystery in which learning the town gives you a chance to help a person you could not help on the first pass. **Differentiation hypothesis:** the combination of a quick browser entry, a visible repeated event, an earned spare action, and a recognizable changed outcome can deliver a compact planning payoff. Time loops, journals, remembered clues and extra actions are not unique separately.

**Signature moment to test:** “I saw why her route failed yesterday; I used the saved minute to change it today, and I can see where she ended up.” This is proposed player language, not a quote or observed response.

Bound the slice to **Bell Square, Mina and Brann, the Dawn→Noon event, and two loops**. Use the existing flower/crate/market motifs. The narrative designer must settle one internally consistent cause (for example, a crowd closes Mina's alley at Noon; observing the blockage lets the player prepare a different route before the next Noon). The normal Noon event must visibly happen whether the forecast was correct, wrong or blank. Distinguish an observed clue from a forecast/theory. In loop two, one clue-dependent intervention changes the outcome; the existing borrowed visit lets the player fit that intervention alongside a normal expedition action. Keep both the successful result and the missed opportunity legible and recoverable. Do not require two carried tokens as extra hidden keys.

Use the current Contrast build as the primary comparison, because Original also differs in journal, batching and skip behavior. Preserve an immutable Original reference. A first formative session should ask for a prediction and a reason before the player chooses, without teaching the solution. Observing the earlier event must have a purpose beyond finding a highlighted button.

**System scope:** one event state transition, one provenance-bearing clue flag, one conditional action, one outcome record, one small journal receipt. Preserve atomic Momentum spend, once-per-loop rule, locked forecasts, no duplicate rewards, Fracture override, restart and mode isolation. No general schedule simulation, new district, new currency or generalized inventory puzzle system.

**Art/interaction scope:** retain the canon map; create two small Bell Square scene states plus one changed-outcome inset using the same recognizable Mina silhouette, crate, redflower and alley landmarks. A limited map badge and short boundary transition should show where the person went; text labels must convey it without color or motion. One optional bell/route cue may support the beat, with mute and reduced-motion equivalents. No full-town repaint, portrait roster or cinematic. Place current clue and relevant cost near the action; test focus/scroll behavior on the long phone page.

**Predeclared formative gate, not a statistical claim:** six new participants, three per first-build order, with order recorded. At least four explain the witnessed cause and predict the intervention's consequence before receiving its result; at least four use the earned action without coaching and explain why Fracture can still end the day; at least three later name Mina's specific changed outcome rather than only “more Momentum.” Record help requests, wrong forecasts, time spent rereading, missed-opportunity response and voluntary retry. Zero repeatable duplicate-reward, wrong-state or inaccessible-action failures. If participants simply memorize the answer, follow a highlight without an explanation, or recall only the meter, revise the slice rather than add content. Six sessions guide design; they do not prove retention or superiority.
