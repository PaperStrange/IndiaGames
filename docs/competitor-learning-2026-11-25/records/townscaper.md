# Townscaper — erasure can create a new form

**Session:** agent-operated official [web demo](https://oskarstalberg.com/Townscaper/), 25 November 2026. Chromium 151.0.7922.173, Linux, 1280×900, screenshot-guided programmed mouse input. No game-state inspection, injected outcomes, cheats, walkthrough or review reading before play. Valid TLS retained. This is a bounded firsthand observation, not a human participant, physical-device test, full-game completion or evidence of long-term enjoyment.

**Selection signal:** the [commercial Steam product](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1291340/Townscaper/) showed **95% positive from 10,650 English reviews**, with **88% positive from 42 recent reviews**, retrieved 25 November. These are Steam's displayed categories, not a score for this web demo or a universal ranking. A cached curator URL returned different counts; the plain product URL was separately fetched and used. Reviews were read after closing the game.

## Declared scope and actual endpoint

Before opening: build a neighborhood, attempt a chosen architectural feature, remove a supporting/adjacent block, then revise; stop at that boundary or 20 active observation minutes. The action log spans **02:23:32–02:30:33 UTC**, about seven wall-clock minutes including startup, screenshots, tool delays and notes. Active play time was not separately measured. There are 50 logged UI/navigation/wait/capture operations and 19 screenshots, not 50 gameplay decisions.

**Completed bounded journey:** extended the starting town; built a cyan house, then a two-storey turret; removed its lower storey and observed a generated stilt cabin; corrected an unintended island and rebuilt the house. No authored ending exists in the observed scope. Saving/reopening, other architectural rules, large builds, touch, audio and later return remain untested.

## Implications for our four demos — hypotheses to test

**Notebook:** preserve real ink, erasure and recovery, but test whether removing part of a stroke changes a relationship or behavior. A strong experiment would let the same drawn object become a bridge, channel or shelter through edits; another should remain mechanically different. The important observed principle is productive revision, not copying houses or selecting a new dialogue option. This session does not select a final core.

**Vivid:** compare a scene where moving/removing a frame element changes a visible character situation against the existing read-and-reveal baseline. Record whether the player notices and can explain the changed situation, then chooses a second purposeful edit. Attractive 2.5D decoration alone would not test that hypothesis.

**Small Hours:** test one partner transforming the other's contribution in the shared artifact, with a reversible edit and visible credit. Townscaper was solo here; it supplies no evidence that this will produce pair intimacy or comfortable cooperation.

**Bell:** use hover/preview and distinct committed-action feedback to show what an intervention targets. Do not replace causal stakes with an unconstrained sandbox merely because Townscaper is highly rated.

A concrete next comparison should capture unaided first action, explanation of its consequence, self-stated next goal, an intentional revision and a chosen stopping reason. Qualitative failure cases matter as much as completion. No threshold or novelty claim is proven by this single agent session.
