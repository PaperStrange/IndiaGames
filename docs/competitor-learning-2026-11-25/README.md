# Learning from play — 25 November 2026

**Research before redesign.** This round records six official-browser attempts across material play, construction, automation, platforming, rhythm and character choices. Five reached substantive gameplay; ADOFAI remained at calibration/menu. These are agent observations, followed by public player reviews and discussions. They are not human qualification, full-game reviews or a market-demand estimate. No revised game sequence, new demo or additional development preview was implemented.

## The main learning

**Change the decision, not just the wording or gesture.** Sort's recurring characters and resource tradeoffs, shapez's routing problems, Sandspiel's material interactions and Townscaper's constructive erasure provide different ways to create a next question. Their own players still report repetition, opaque rules or absent purpose. A highly rated game can succeed for an audience while leaving another audience cold.

For our three reported problems:

| Owner-reported problem | Next question to test |
|---|---|
| Hard to understand how to play or why | Can a fresh player form an intention, act and explain both the consequence and why it matters? A completed instructed route cannot answer this. |
| Flat character/story loop | Does the next beat change what the player must notice, infer or alter? A new portrait and paragraph are insufficient evidence. |
| Ordinary interaction and missing previews | Does the player's act materially change a persistent situation, with legible recovery? Separately, can they reach the exact playable build online? |

[The synthesis](SYNTHESIS.md) answers **why play / how to differentiate / what distinctive experience to test** separately for all four games. Its proposed changes remain hypotheses, including for Notebook. It also supplies a concrete revised sequence and human-observation questions; none of those experiments has been implemented in this research round.
