## Three product questions: hypotheses to test

| Game | Why might people play? | Proposed differentiation | Unusual experience to test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small Hours | Low-pressure connection and making something together. | A brief private-to-shared ritual with an optional local artifact. | Two perspectives become one personally meaningful keepsake: observe both people's contributions and voluntary saving. |
| Vivid | Curiosity, gentle competence and emotional discovery through objects. | Short, accessible stories where manipulation expresses a relationship. | A small act of care becomes a persistent arrangement; ask what the player's action changed and why it mattered. |
| Bell | Learning a place well enough to change someone's outcome. | Witness-derived knowledge changes a later intervention. | The town remembers what the player learned: test causal explanation and neutral recall, not route imitation. |

These are aspirations, not proven uniqueness, demand or retention. Complete a feasible comparison from a different family where coverage remains shallow; distinguish browser editions, desk research and partials. Research breadth is useful, but is not a new launch-policy threshold.

Keep the existing **84 task objects / 47 completed** unchanged. Completed engineering stays complete within its recorded scope. The [published proposal][plan] requires **English, Japanese and Simplified Chinese for every November 20 release; incomplete games defer**. Desktop/phone/tablet targets still need exact hardware, fluent reviewers and a settled locale-evidence contract. Existing cohorts/thresholds remain; additional locale sampling is undecided. New translated/font/layout bytes require new immutable candidates and exact-build evidence. Reconcile participant/session provenance and gate eligibility, then settle implementation priorities together.
